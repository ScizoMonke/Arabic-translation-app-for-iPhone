D2 (2026-10-31, decided, user) Dialect: Egyptian Arabic in both directions. Confirmed by the user's father; the family being visited is in Egypt.
D3 (2026-10-31, decided, user) Top half of the screen: Arabic speech heard around the user becomes English text. Quality bar in the user's words: "speak very few word still understand". Broken English is acceptable if it is comprehensible.
D4 (2026-10-31, decided, user) Bottom half: English the user speaks becomes the Egyptian Arabic way to say the same thing, written in Latin letters as pronunciation. Not a translation into Arabic script. User: "They just need to be able to understand me."
D5 (2026-10-31, decided, user) No Arabic script is shown anywhere in the app.
D6 (2026-10-31, decided, user) Battery drain is accepted. The app is used only when needed.

D12 (2026-10-31, decided, user) Departure is in 5 days, about 2026-11-05. No fine-tuning or model conversion pipeline fits; the app assembles models that exist today. Day 1 goes to proving an IPA reaches the phone at all.
D13 (2026-10-31, decided, user) The bottom half must also show what the Arabic he is about to say actually means, in plain English, so he knows exactly what he is saying. User: "I need to be able to know what it is exactly that I'm going to be saying. So it just must be written in English." Bottom half therefore shows three things: the English the phone heard him say, the romanized Egyptian to say, and a literal English gloss of that Egyptian.
D14 (2026-10-31, decided, user) Overfitting to this single task is permitted and wanted. User: "we can really overfit here. Because we don't care of nothing else but this one thing." A pre-vetted phrasebook layer in front of the language model is in scope.
D15 (2026-10-31, decided, user) The user's father will not grade output. Verification of the bottom half is by cross-checking: a second model back-translates the Arabic to English, and the orchestrator, who reads Arabic but is not a native Egyptian speaker, audits samples. This is weaker than a native grader and is recorded as such.
D16 (2026-10-31, decided, orchestrator) Top half mechanism: WhisperKit running Whisper on the phone with the translate task, producing English directly from Arabic speech. Verified 2026-10-31 by reading raw source: Sources/WhisperKit/Core/Models.swift declares DecodingTask with cases transcribe and translate. Apple's Translation framework (Arabic listed, on device, per ledger) is the fallback route from an Arabic transcript, checked for availability at runtime rather than assumed.
D17 (2026-10-31, open, orchestrator) Bottom half model: chosen today by a bench on this PC among Nile-Chat-4B (MBZUAI-Paris, Gemma-3 base, trained on Egyptian Arabic in Latin script), NileChat-3B (UBC-NLP, Qwen2.5 base) and Qwen3-4B. Criterion: correctness and naturalness of romanized Egyptian for everyday family-visit sentences, judged by reading, then whether the architecture runs under MLX Swift on the phone.
D18 (2026-10-31, decided, executor choice ratified by orchestrator) Whisper variant on the phone is openai_whisper-large-v3_947MB, not the large-v3 turbo variant named in D16. OpenAI's whisper README states turbo "is not trained for translation tasks" and "will return the original language even if --task translate is specified"; Argmax marks turbo as recommended on macOS and does not list it for the iPhone 17 family. Amends D16. Effects: about 950 MB of model on the phone, slower decoding than turbo, and the PC bench measures large-v3 for translation.
D19 (2026-10-31, decided, executor choice ratified) Routing between halves: WhisperKit language detection runs on every utterance; "en" goes to the bottom half (transcribe), anything else to the top half (translate, source forced to Arabic). The Them/Me switch bypasses detection. Apple's speech recognizer is not used. Reason: only two languages are in the room.
D17 (2026-10-31, decided, orchestrator; closes the open D17) Bottom-half model: Nile-Chat-4B (MBZUAI-Paris, Gemma-3 base). Evidence from bench/llm/runs on 40 family-visit sentences, read by the orchestrator in Arabic: the Arabic-script field was right or acceptable in 36, wrong in 2 (34 "turn left" came out as turn right; 36 "stop here" came out as don't walk here), and formal rather than colloquial in 2 (8, 16). Qwen3-4B answered mostly in Modern Standard Arabic or Levantine. Nile-Chat's own Latin and gloss fields were unreliable (digits in the Latin field, glosses that copied the Arabizi), which drives D20.
D20 (2026-10-31, decided, orchestrator) The bottom half is three stages, not one. (1) The model is asked only for Egyptian in Arabic script plus Arabizi, its two native outputs, as strict JSON. (2) The Latin respelling the user reads is computed from the Arabizi by a fixed digit map (2 and 3 become an apostrophe, 5 kh, 7 h, 8 gh, 9 s, 6 t), never by the model. (3) The English gloss is Apple's on-device translation of the Arabic-script field back into English: an independent engine, so a wrong translation shows up as a wrong gloss, which is what D13 is for. Amends D10: no stressed-syllable capitals, because stress is not derivable; plain lowercase respelling.
D21 (2026-10-31, decided, orchestrator) On-phone runtime for the model: llama.cpp as a Swift package, loading a 4-bit GGUF of Nile-Chat-4B published by a third-party quantizer (mradermacher/Nile-Chat-4B-GGUF), downloaded once at home like the Whisper model. Reason: no MLX-format conversion exists on Hugging Face, and making one needs a Mac plus somewhere to host 2.5 GB; the GGUF exists today. MLX Swift is the fallback only if the llama.cpp package fights the cloud build. The quantized file is run on this PC through Ollama on the same 40 sentences before it ships.
D22 (2026-10-31, decided, user D14 applied) A phrasebook of about 200 family-visit phrases sits in front of the model: the heard English is matched against it first, and only unmatched sentences go to the model. Authored by an Opus executor, cross-checked by back-translation with a different model, audited by the orchestrator. It is also the regression set for the bottom half.
D23 (2026-10-31, decided, orchestrator) Decoding on the phone: greedy, no repetition penalty (repeat_penalty 1.0), at most 120 new tokens, stop when the JSON object closes. Measured on the exact 4-bit file through Ollama (bench/llm/runs): with repeat_penalty 1.15 the two-field prompt collapsed in 21 of 40 rows into escaped junk and copied examples; at 1.0 it parsed in 39 of 40 with one repetition loop. Loop guard: abort when one character repeats more than eight times in a row, retry once with mild sampling (temperature 0.3, top_k 20); on a second failure show that the model could not answer and log it.
D24 (2026-10-31, decided, orchestrator) Prompt rules added to the two-field prompt: the speaker is a man talking to family, so first-person forms are masculine (row 39 came back feminine); the arabizi field is Latin letters, digits and spaces only (rows 7, 23 and 35 put Arabic script there). Parser validation: the arabic field must contain Arabic-range characters and the arabizi field none; a failed validation triggers the same single retry as D23, then a crude letter-by-letter transliteration of the Arabic field marked as rough.
D25 (2026-10-31, decided, orchestrator) Top half stays Whisper large-v3 with the translate task (D16, D18). Evidence in bench/asr/runs/mgb3: on 30 Egyptian broadcast clips, large-v3 translate produced English that the orchestrator judged comprehensible on 7 of 8 clips read in full against the Arabic reference, with the gist right and one or two wrong words per clip. Egyptian fine-tunes transcribe with WER 0.33 to 0.39 against 0.40 for stock large-v3; the best score (0.24) is suspected of training on this very dataset. Converting a fine-tune to Core ML needs a Mac-runner day for a modest gain, so it is declined for this trip and may be revisited after.
D26 (2026-10-31, decided, orchestrator) Phrasebook v1 accepted: 272 entries, 1,094 aliases, at app/EgyptTalk/Resources/phrasebook.json, built from bench/phrasebook/entries.py. The orchestrator read 56 entries (four per scene) in Arabic and found no dialect errors and literal glosses throughout. The second-model cross-check (qwen3:8b) flagged 62 entries, almost all of them the judge misreading everyday Egyptian (water as hundred, bread as living); 9 real ambiguities it did catch were fixed by the author and are listed in bench/phrasebook/account.md.
D27 (2026-10-31, inferred, veto open) When a phrase changes form by the listener's gender, one entry shows both, man first: "ashoofak bokra / ashoofek bokra", with the gloss ending "(to a man / to a woman)". Reason: he speaks to uncles and aunts in the same hour, and the alternative is two entries he must pick between before speaking. Costs a longer line on screen.
D28 (2026-10-31, decided, executor choice ratified) llama.cpp enters the app as upstream's prebuilt xcframework (release b12020, SHA-256 pinned in app/LlamaBinary/Package.swift), not as a source package: the brief's premise that llama.cpp ships a Package.swift was wrong, and the binary route is the one upstream documents. Exact alias matches are unaffected.

D34 (2026-10-31, decided, user; supersedes D11 and D19) Who is speaking is no longer detected. The top half listens at all times and treats everything it hears as Arabic to translate into English. The bottom half works only while the user presses and holds: audio captured during the hold goes to the bottom pipeline only, the top half ignores it, and release ends the utterance. D35 (2026-10-31, decided, orchestrator) The user's own English is recognized by Apple's on-device English speech recognition, streaming, shown live in the "heard" line while he holds; Whisper is reserved for the Arabic. Reason: speed. User's measurement on the phone: "about 3 to 5 seconds for the English to Arab pronunciation translation". Apple's English recognizer is fast and streaming; the Whisper large-v3 pass was a large share of those seconds.
D36 (2026-10-31, decided, orchestrator) Top-half failure seen by the user: "I see what they spoke in the Arabic pronunciation, but it's not translated to English." That is Whisper's translate task emitting Arabic in Latin letters instead of English. Fix in two layers: an English prefix prompt to the decoder to bias output to English, and a language check on the output (Apple's NaturalLanguage framework); when the output is not English, the app runs a transcribe pass and translates the Arabic with Apple Translation. The top half shows English or nothing; Latin-script Arabic never reaches the top view.
D37 (2026-10-31, decided, orchestrator) The language model's fixed prompt prefix is processed once into the KV cache at load and reused for every sentence; only the sentence and the answer are decoded per request. Reason: prompt processing of the roughly 250-token prefix was paid on every utterance.
